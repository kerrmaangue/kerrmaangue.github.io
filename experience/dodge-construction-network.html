Dodge Construction Network (DCN)

Role: National Public Planning Reporter / Remote Project Controls Specialist · Years: 2018–2023

Five years of remote work turning public planning documents into accurate, deduplicated project records.

Public planning sources I tracked

Projects start in public documents long before bidding. These are the sources I followed.

Capital Improvement Plans (CIPs)

Multi-year lists of planned public projects and budgets. I tracked them to surface projects early.

State Revolving Funds (SRF)

State-administered loan programs for water and wastewater work, another early signal of public projects.

Bond referendums

Voter-approved bonds that fund school and municipal construction, tracked from approval onward.

Municipal agendas

Council and board agendas, where projects, bids and awards first appear in public.

Keeping the record clean

Once a project was found, the work was making its record complete and correct.

Project setup (V1 reports)

Setting up each new project as a Dodge Report (V1) with its core details.

RFI / submittal logs

Logging RFIs and submittals so every question and approval has a record.

A/E & GC factor verification

Verifying the architect/engineer and general contractor tied to a project.

Project deduplication

Finding and merging duplicate records so each project appears once.

Quality control

Records were held to DCN quality control standards. That habit of checking sources, verifying parties and removing duplicates is what I bring to client trackers and logs.

How it carries into client work

  • RFI and submittal logging becomes the RFI and submittal logs I keep for clients.
  • Deduplication means one clean record per project or document, not three versions.
  • Source tracking becomes bid, permit and approval tracking on your project.
